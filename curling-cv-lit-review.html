1. Verdict in one screen

The objective, as stated, is a multi-year research programme. The term-sized version of it is not hard — it is narrow.

The literal goal ("take any curling video, from any angle, and produce accurate detailed analysis") requires solving unconstrained monocular camera geometry, occluded small-object detection, identity association, collision resolution and a century-old unresolved physics problem — all at once. Nobody has done that, and the strong published work does it only for constrained broadcast camera families.

The good news is unusual for a research project: almost every individual component already has a public benchmark, a public dataset, or a published baseline with a number attached. You are never starting from nothing, and almost every target is already quantified, so you always know what "good" means.

The bad news is concentrated in three places (§7), and two of them are genuinely unclaimed.

The one sentence to remember

The perception layers are mostly solved, the reasoning layer is completely solved, the LLM layer is feasible, and the single thing nobody has published is the validated bridge between video and the sport's own official shot-by-shot records. That bridge is where the contribution is.

This is a rapid structured sweep, not a systematic review. Broad searches across curling-specific CV, general sports-broadcast geometry, sports multi-object tracking, sports event detection, curling physics, curling analytics, LLM commentary, video-to-simulation, and wheelchair/Paralympic coverage. Nothing here was read beyond abstract/summary depth except where noted, so treat the numbers as reported, not reproduced.

3. The three layers

Every design in this space decomposes the same way. Difficulty is wildly uneven across the layers, which is the single most useful fact for scoping.

A · Perception
video → canonical state
hard

Rink registration, stone detection, tracking, rotation, world coordinates, collisions.

→
B · State and reasoning
state → judgement
solved

Win probability, expected score, shot choice, optimal strategy. Rich public baselines.

→
C · Language
judgement → words
feasible

Commentary, coaching feedback, Q&A. Small, evaluable, and where the LLM belongs.

Why the layering matters for a one-term project

Layer B is fully deliverable and gradeable with zero video — official game records plus public simulator baselines. Layer C is deliverable on top of B. Only Layer A is risky. So the project can succeed even if perception fails, provided the layers are kept genuinely separate. That separation is the architecture's real value — more than the elegance of any "game world" framing.

5. Evidence charts

6. Component-by-component

1 · Rink registration from an arbitrary view Borrow

Recovering the mapping from pixels to sheet coordinates is the foundational step and it is a mature field with its own survey literature and standard recipe: detect markings → build correspondences → RANSAC + DLT homography → refine. Soccer is most developed; ice hockey is the closest analogue because it is a rink rather than a pitch.

Reusable artefacts: HockeyRink (661 frames, 56 annotated rink keypoints, pretrained model + live demo); 4,262 hockey frames with homographies; ice-rink localization (ResNet-18 regressing four control points, Hann-window temporal smoothing to kill jitter); curated bibliography; PnLCalib (joint point+line optimisation, code released); TVCalib (learns camera pose and focal length, not just a homography).

Directly applicable precedent: Hockey2D uses YOLO rink keypoints + RANSAC homography, and critically filters frames with a shot-type classifier so homography is only computed on suitable scenes. That is the same architecture recommended here, already published for a rink-based sport.

Where it is still open: central and close-up views with sparse visible markings (Magera et al., CVPRW 2025), viewpoint generalisation, and jitter under pan/zoom. Curling is favourable here: the sheet carries painted hog lines, tee lines, centre line and four concentric rings per end, so it is marking-rich compared with grass.

2–3 · Stone detection Solved clean Open occluded

The hardest physical fact in the perception layer: a curling stone is roughly 1:146 of sheet scale, and 48.4% of stones are smaller than 32×32 pixels in full-rink views. Occlusion by brooms, player bodies and other stones is the dominant failure mode. Multiple papers identify missed detections under occlusion as the central problem.

Benchmark: the Curling / Curling_hard pair (9,000 clean + 2,000 hard images) is the de-facto standard — but see table 2, it does not appear to be publicly downloadable.

State of the art: IFCD (inverted feature extraction, delayed downsampling, four-scale decoupled head) and SR-Yolo both reach 0.974 mAP@0.5 clean and 0.723 occluded — the 25-point cliff in Chart 1.

Transferable techniques from general small-object detection: delay downsampling or add shallow P2 pyramid layers; multi-scale/adaptive feature fusion; attention for contextual reasoning; Normalised Wasserstein Distance losses to reduce small-box positional sensitivity; tracking-based gap filling.

4 · Stone tracking and identity Borrow

Detection is not the bottleneck here — association is. Stones have coloured handles (red/yellow) so they are "similar yet distinguishable", which is precisely the property that makes the SportsMOT benchmark hard: 8 stones share each colour, and the interesting analysis depends on knowing which stone is which across a whole end.

Ceiling is high: SportsMOT methods now reach HOTA 74–87, with SAM-based tracking-by-segmentation leading. Curling-specific work already hits MOTA 82.87 with 19 ID switches, and CurlObserver adds NSA Kalman filtering with multidimensional matching.

Practical implication: identity persistence across a full end is the part worth measuring, because it is what makes a shot-by-shot log possible at all.

5 · Rotation and handle orientation Borrow

Rotation is not a nice-to-have: turn direction (in-turn vs out-turn) is a formal part of the sport's own record-keeping, and curl direction depends on it. Two established extraction routes exist.

Marker / mark tracking: ceiling cameras with a calibration plate, tracking a black mark on the handle to derive rotation angle over time — measurement error in linking camera fields of ±6 mm. Rotation is near-linear until the stone stops, so a single angular velocity per delivery is a reasonable approximation (ω measured across 0.3–3.0 rad/s).

Handle-orientation fitting: in broadcast, fit the handle line with a Hough transform on top of a detector, or use keypoint detection. Since the stone is axially symmetric about the handle, centre + handle angle fully describe its instantaneous motion state. There is even a patent covering handle-keypoint → angular velocity.

Known limitation: angle detection degrades at steeply tilted camera angles — the same regime where projection accuracy drops.

6–7 · World coordinates, trajectories and collisions Borrow Open collisions

Trajectory extraction from broadcast is done, repeatedly and well. The collision regime is where everything degrades, and it is exactly where the stones cluster — in the house, where you need precision most.

Numbers: CurlObserver — 3.62 cm RMSE, 98.7% detection, 93.4% tracking success, from uncalibrated dynamic perspectives. The 2026 unified RL framework — 2.59 cm mean Hausdorff against real top-view video, but 1.39 cm for non-collision shots and 4.98 cm for collision shots. A fixed-camera YOLO + ByteTrack system reports ~0.02 m average position error and validates hog-line speed against a laser velocimeter.

Interpretation: collision shots are 3.6× worse and land right at the edge of coaching relevance. Practical response: detect and flag collisions rather than claim accuracy through them.

8 · Shot segmentation and camera-shot typing Borrow

The "cut the video into throws" step has a mature literature and should not be researched from scratch. Broadcast sports video has standard tasks: shot boundary detection, camera-shot-type segmentation, and replay detection.

Benchmarks and numbers: SoccerNet-v2 defines 13 camera classes (main camera, close-up player, behind goal, spider-cam, public, …) with transition types {abrupt, logo, smooth}; segmentation reaches 47.3 mIoU with CALF, while simple histogram differencing still achieves ~78.5% mAP on boundary detection. SportSBD fine-tunes a single R(2+1)D 3D CNN that generalises across hockey and soccer, and is open-source.

Why it matters here: a camera-shot-type classifier is what stops replays being counted as new throws, and what lets you route each clip to a method that suits its geometry. Hockey2D already uses one for exactly this routing purpose.

Shot-type classification itself is a published, if modest, result: a Korean study classified 763 real match shots into 5 classes using CNN + Temporal Transformer, with class AUC 0.773–0.938 (Guard best). Note the gap: that work used top-view training footage, and the broadcast work does tracking without semantics. Nothing combines the two across arbitrary angles.

9 · video → official shot-by-shot state Research — the gap

This is the bridge, and it is the most valuable thing here because it is the least done. The perception literature produces trajectories; the analytics literature consumes state; almost nobody validates a reconstruction against the sport's own official records.

Closest existing work: a 2026 paper extracts stone positions from sheet images for match-database construction using YOLO11n with pseudo-label semi-supervised learning, producing 205,364 stone positions across 31 tournaments from 108,114 sheet images. Note the scope: sheet images (top-down diagrams), not broadcast video, and its own group also builds expected-score models. This is the group closest to your idea.

What is missing: a paired benchmark of (broadcast video, officially recorded shot state). That is constructible for free by selecting games whose official shot-by-shot records exist, and it would be the first of its kind — a genuine methodological contribution with rigorous ground truth.

10 · Curl physics and trajectory prediction Research

A century-old open problem. The physical origin of curl is still contested, with competing families: forward–backward friction asymmetry, left–right velocity-dependent asymmetry, scratch-guide, pivot-slide, edge, contact-area, and slow-side selection. Classical symmetric friction models predict zero or reversed curl.

The decisive comparison: best classical mechanistic model RMSE 22.59 cm against 0.999 cm for a learned CasLSTM — a 22× gap, with the physics baseline error nearly equal to the stone's own diameter. Learned and hybrid models also dominate for collision dynamics, where physical models are described as useful only for coarse momentum transfer.

Useful physical constants for sanity checks: stone–ice friction coefficient 0.006–0.016, decreasing with sliding speed; Olympic rink quality parameter α = 0.0087; back-line to button travel time ≈ 26.8 s; rotational decay follows a power law with exponent ≈1.586, not the 2 expected from simple kinetic friction.

Verdict for your project: adjacent, not central. Predicted-resting-position is a clean, quantified target with a clear baseline, but it is a separate project from video understanding.

11 · Strategy and decision support Solved

Rich, well-baselined, and reproducible from public data. Do not present this as a contribution — present it as the layer that consumes your state.

Analysis: three-dimensional Markov win-probability models over (score differential, end, hammer), fitted on 10,933 ends from 1,199 matches. Teams starting with hammer win ≈58% of matches and score ≥1 in ≈68% of their hammer ends; being up one without hammer in the 10th is worth more (56%) than down one with hammer (44%). Blanking an end often beats taking a single point, with analytical thresholds derived.

Modern ML: expected-score-distribution models mapping a board configuration plus game context to a distribution over end scores (−8 to +8), trained on real WCF championship boards, integrated with empirical win-probability tables so decisions maximise win probability rather than expected score. Mixed doubles needs separate models (8 ends, no-removal rules, higher steal frequency).

Simulation and agents: Digital Curling (DC3/DC4) is the de-facto standard platform — a decoupled C++ physics library (FCV), open-source server and Python clients, a documented protocol, and a decade of competition agents (MCTS, DIS, UNIST-SAIL, Kura Curling with a reported 0.93 win rate over its predecessor). DC4 persists shot-by-shot state in a database explicitly for sequence-model dataset construction. There is also a 2026 SAC-based framework with a WCF-rules simulator.

Caution: all high-fidelity simulators leave sweeping as future work — which is a quiet argument for wheelchair curling, where no sweeping is permitted and the gap does not apply.

12 · LLM commentary and coaching Feasible

A live, benchmarked area with a clear evaluation method — and the evidence supports the architecture recommended here.

The key result: "Input Matters" (INLG 2025) hand-annotated 3,312 factual errors across 180 LLM-generated game summaries comparing input formats. JSON input reduced error rates by 69% (Llama-3.1-70B) and 65% (Qwen2.5-72B) versus unstructured text; row-structured input by 54% and 51%. Over 80% of the variance in error rates was attributable to input structure.

Field-level risks: hallucination is the top deployment concern in sports LLMs, because invented events, actors and causal links are plausible enough to mislead real decisions. Latency is the second concern (one soccer pipeline reports ~3.1 s per sentence, with the LLM stage the bottleneck).

Design implication: feed the model a structured metric vector, never pixels, for anything factual. That both matches the empirical evidence and removes the class of error the LLM is worst at.

7. The three open problems

Everything hard in this project reduces to one of these three. Everything else has a recipe.

(a) The bridge — video to validated official state Open

No published work validates a video-derived shot-by-shot game state against the sport's own official records. The closest effort extracts stone positions from top-down sheet diagrams, not broadcast video. This is the gap, and it is constructible: pick games whose official shot-by-shot records exist, and you have paired video and true state for free.

Why it is tractable: the ground truth already exists and is free; the metric is obvious (position error in cm per shot); and partial success is still a result because the reasoning layer is unaffected.

(b) Occlusion and collision resolution Open

A 25-point mAP cliff from clean to occluded scenes, and a 3.6× worse trajectory error on collision shots. Both failure modes concentrate exactly where the analysis is most valuable — the house, with stones clustered, brooms and bodies in the way. Public benchmarks exist for the detection half, so this is measurable, but it is nowhere near solved.

Recommended posture: flag, do not solve. Detect when the system is inside a degraded regime and report confidence, rather than publishing a single averaged number that hides it.

(c) Curl physics Open

Contested for roughly a century; classical models fail to reproduce curl at all. But learned models are already 22× better than the best physical model, so the practical path is closed — only the explanatory path is open. Adjacent to your project rather than central.

8. What is already taken

Two findings from this sweep materially change the risk picture. Both are about competition, not capability.

Wheelchair curling CV is occupied — possibly the most competitive corner of the field

If your client's need is wheelchair curling analysis, you are entering a space with a well-resourced incumbent:

  • A full pipeline (situation-aware detection + curling MCTS decision engine) was evaluated with China's national wheelchair curling team and credited with their Beijing 2022 Paralympic gold. It also released a curling detection dataset.
  • Dedicated Chinese patents cover wheelchair-curling track recognition, positioning, tracking and motion-state detection.
  • The standard Curling / Curling_hard benchmark datasets were collected at the China Administration of Sports for Persons with Disabilities.
  • A curling robot has played official matches against a national wheelchair curling reserve team.
  • Ice-quality measurement has already been performed on video from the World Wheelchair Curling Championships.
  • A 2025 study on eye-tracking and delivery accuracy in elite wheelchair curling classified expertise with 90% accuracy and describes itself as among the first ML work on para-athlete cognitive markers.

Reading: baselines, datasets and domain expertise exist — good for feasibility, bad for novelty. The honest framing is "we extend/validate", never "we are first". And note the mismatch risk: the publicly available and widely cited datasets in this space are able-bodied or are held by Chinese institutions, so if the client wants wheelchair curling you may be building on the wrong domain's data.

The bridge is being approached from the other direction

A 2026 paper builds a curling match database by extracting 205,364 stone positions across 31 tournaments from 108,114 sheet images. The same research group also builds expected-score models from real WCF boards. They are converging on the state representation from the diagram side; the video side of the bridge appears unclaimed. That is both the opportunity and the clock: this is an active group.

9. The ontology you must adopt

This is the most actionable section, and the easiest thing for a CV-first team to miss. The sport already has a formal, encoded shot taxonomy and grading scheme. Your intermediate representation should conform to it rather than inventing a schema — it is what makes your outputs comparable to official records, and what makes "did the analysis agree with the officials?" answerable.

Four details that will otherwise bite you

  • Grade the intention, not the outcome. The official guidance is explicit: record the task the skip called. A flushed take-out that happens to work is still a miss. If your system grades outcomes it will systematically disagree with official data — and, worse, with coaches.
  • Handle is recorded per shot — clockwise (in-turn) or counter-clockwise (out-turn). Player handedness is irrelevant; only stone rotation matters. This is why §6.5 rotation extraction is not optional.
  • No bonus points. The ceiling is 100%. Some older commentary about 5- or 6-point shots is out of date.
  • There is no degree-of-difficulty adjustment in the standard system: a simple peel and a double raise take-out can both score 4. If you want a difficulty-aware metric you are proposing a new one, and you should say so plainly and validate it.

Note also the interpretation caveat the sport itself admits: "the lead shot 78%" is really "the team shot 78% on the first two shots of each end" — stats are aggregated by position, and different statisticians disagree on individual shots. That subjectivity is a real threat to your evaluation, and quantifying it (inter-annotator agreement) is itself a contribution.

10. Recommended one-term scope

Given one term, no guaranteed ice access, arbitrary-source video, and a supervisor who is also a founder: pick one claim and treat everything else as borrowed scaffolding.

Claim to defend

"Can a monocular system reconstruct curling shot state that agrees with the sport's official records, and can an LLM turn that state into coaching feedback that coaches endorse?"

That is one sentence, it contains two measurable sub-claims, and it degrades gracefully — the second sub-claim survives even if the first half-fails.

What to build

PhaseWeeksOutputStatus

Cut list — say these out loud in the scope document

Real-time processing · full-match analysis · multi-camera fusion · sweeping analysis · body-pose/form analysis · MCTS strategy search · solving curl physics · state-of-the-art detection on the occluded benchmark. Each is defensible as "future work", and each would otherwise consume a term on its own.

Bail conditions — decide these before week 1

  1. No usable footage of any kind by end of week 2 → pivot to the layer-B-only project (reasoning + commentary on official records). It still satisfies the supervisor's analysis goal and cannot fail.
  2. Calibration error worse than ~5 cm by end of week 4 → narrow the claim to relative comparisons and shift the emphasis onto the LLM study.
  3. Fewer than ~100 usable shots by week 6 → drop per-tier reporting and run a single-tier evaluation.

11. The three-data-path discipline

The one methodological trap that will silently invalidate results in this architecture: training on your own reconstruction output, then evaluating on it.

PathSourceAllowed useNever
TrainOfficial game records (60,536 shots); simulator rollouts (DC3/DC4, unlimited)Fit the reasoning layer—
TestHeld-out official records; hand-labelled video with independently verified stateReport the headline numbers—
Error analysisYour own video → state outputDiagnose the converter; per-tier breakdownsTraining or reporting metrics here

And alongside it: simulate the rules, not the ice. Rules and strategy are discrete and robust; physics is continuous and brittle, and every high-fidelity simulator omits sweeping. Use simulation for the game tree, and real records for anything that has to be true of real ice.

12. Questions for the supervisor

Take these to the next meeting. Each one changes the project's shape, and none can be answered from the literature.

  1. Who is the client, and what is the actual job — broadcast overlay, coaching feedback, or automatic clipping? These are three different products. (Note: AI stone-tracking with real-time path/speed/rotation graphics and automatic highlight generation is already being deployed by the official Olympic broadcaster for the 2026 Games, so the broadcast-enhancement framing is commercially occupied.)
  2. Able-bodied or wheelchair curling? This changes the confound structure (no sweeping in wheelchair curling), the technique analysis, the available datasets, and — per §8 — the novelty risk substantially.
  3. Can the client supply footage, or ice access? The most valuable question in the meeting. A commercial partner may unlock what a university club cannot.
  4. What is the term deliverable — a working system on N videos, or a benchmark plus report? For one term, the second.
  5. Which single claim matters most — measurement accuracy, arbitrary-video robustness, or LLM reasoning? All three cannot be chased at once, and asking for "as general as possible" is how honours projects end with a demo and no evaluation.

Then get the answer in writing: the research question, the claims, and what is explicitly not being attempted and why. With a supervisor who is also a founder, the client's roadmap and the dissertation's requirements will pull apart, and you will be the one caught in the middle.
