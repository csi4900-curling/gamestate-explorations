# Possible camera edits below the frozen hard-cut threshold

The [China plan](possible-edits-v1-plan.json) fixes a second-tier frame-change diagnostic before its full-source scan: at 5 Hz, propose a **possible edit** when the mean greyscale change is at least 60 and HSV histogram distance is at least 0.25. Existing conservative hard cuts require 45 and 0.35. Proposals within 0.3 s of an existing hard cut are excluded from the extra set. The same rule and hash checks were frozen in a [Switzerland plan](../swiss-full-transfer/possible-edits-v1-plan.json) before scanning that recording. Neither plan uses event labels or counter colours.

Both complete scans reproduced every timestamp and sample count in their frozen hard-cut artifacts before writing results, and a second full run reproduced both JSON outputs exactly. The selected China cases at **1606.0 s** in `S123` and **7029.4 s** in `S519` appear in the 58 proposals; their candidate cap transits were studied separately. A deterministic random sample of 12 China proposals (seed `20261008`; times 519.6, 693.6, 1134.0, 1547.4, 1624.6, 2360.2, 2583.0, 2837.0, 4029.2, 5382.0, 5515.8 and 6967.0 s) visibly switches camera views in all 12 reviewed before/after pairs. In the six Swiss proposals, source inspection shows clear edits but also foreground occlusion or subject movement without a clear view edit. This is developer review for diagnosis, not independent cut precision or recall.

The [postscan event overlap](possible-edit-event-overlap-postscan.json) keeps the event ledger unchanged. **35** of the 112 candidates in the specific frozen cap-fusion ledger occupy shots containing a possible edit. **29** proposed edits fall inside an existing same-shot cap-track time interval, across **28** camera shots; **22** of those edit/track overlaps occur in that cap-fusion ledger's event shots. A track crossing a threshold proposal is an identity risk, not proof that the track is invalid or the delivered stone changed. The separate `S123` and `S519` fallback cues are not events in this particular cap-fusion ledger.

These results show that the conservative shot manifest can undersegment broadcast view changes, particularly in the China source. They do **not** justify silently splitting all 58 shots: the Swiss sample shows that the looser rule also fires on non-cut visual changes. The appropriate next step is a source-separated edit verifier and an event-level comparison that preserves abstention when a camera boundary is uncertain. A camera edit is not itself a throw event, and no physical throw F1 is established here.

A [postscan registration audit](../possible-edit-registration-postscan.json) checks background-feature transforms across the 64 China/Swiss proposed frame pairs. The existing camera-motion reliability test accepts **0/58 China** and **0/6 Swiss** pairs. That includes visually obvious China cuts **and** Swiss proposals caused by foreground movement or occlusion. Registration failure therefore cannot distinguish a real edit from these non-cut changes and is not promoted to a cut classifier. The China/Swiss full scans and this registration audit reproduce exactly from their hashed inputs.

## Temporal-isolation tier and source-separated transfer

After inspecting selected China/Swiss examples, we fixed a second-tier rule: compare the candidate frame pair with the immediately preceding and following 0.2-second pairs. Call it an **isolated spike** only if both the greyscale-change and histogram-distance ratios to the larger neighbor are at least 2. The [Norway possible-edit plan](../canada-norway-full-transfer/possible-edits-v1-plan.json), [Slovakia possible-edit plan](../slovakia-full-transfer/possible-edits-v1-plan.json), and corresponding temporal plans were hashed before scanning those complete recordings. Their full scans reproduced all 708 and 494 frozen conservative hard cuts respectively. The unchanged rule then yielded **7/10** isolated Norway proposals and **13/17** isolated Slovakia proposals. Both new full scans and both temporal-tier outputs reproduced exactly on second runs. The China/Swiss 55/58 and 3/6 counts above are retrospective development results.

Source-frame review on the new recordings reveals both sides of the tradeoff. Isolated examples include clear broadcast view switches. Nonisolated proposals include ordinary foreground movement, but also genuine view changes through a dissolve in Norway `S325` around 4433.0 s, a side-to-wide switch in Norway `S381` around 5329.2 s, and broadcast transition animations in Slovakia `S168` around 2821.6 s and `S427` around 6553.2 s. Thus temporal isolation is useful for ranking review candidates but **cannot be a hard cut veto**. These judgments are development inspection, not an independent cut benchmark, and no throw-event F1 changes.
