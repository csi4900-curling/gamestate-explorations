# Slovakia–USA isolated-edit transfer

This is a **development transfer run**, not independent throw-event validation. The unchanged Korea–China 2× temporal isolation test passed 13 of 17 previously frozen possible edits in the 7,616 s Slovakia–USA recording. Adding those 13 points to 494 conservative hard cuts produced 508 camera-shot windows instead of 495. The frozen first-pass outputs remain unchanged.

The [cut-effect audit](cut-effect-audit.json) shows seven cuts moving an already-positive origin into the right-hand window (`101`) and six leaving both child windows negative (`000`). None converted a negative original window into a positive one. Yet the full event ledger increased from **119 to 122 candidates**: visual-only candidates rose from 25 to 28, while counter-backed candidates stayed at **94** and four counter updates still lacked candidates. The [ledger comparison](ledger-comparison.json) records seven counter-backed window changes; none changes colour. B57's release window becomes *wider* (4385.8–4391.0 s to 4383.0–4422.2 s), so a cut is not automatically a timing improvement.

Four augmented visual-only candidates have no overlapping first-pass candidate windows, while one first-pass visual-only candidate has no augmented overlap. Those are **changed proposals**, not independently established gained or lost throws. Some are far from the added cut times because later motion selection and fusion depend on the global shot sequence. The [multiscale handoff](multiscale-event-handoff.json) preserves the 94 counter-backed hypotheses and both sets of visual-only proposals without forcing physical identity.

These results, together with the Canada–Norway transfer and Korea–China development run, argue against promoting isolated image changes directly to accepted camera cuts or treating a higher candidate count as recall. Source-timestamped independent throw labels are still needed for precision, recall and F1; game states and physics simulation are not verified by these outputs.

A separate [5 Hz patch audit](visual-only-patch-evidence.json) retains all 28 visual-only proposals and scores cap-track appearance using a frozen model. Fifteen have at least one track with stone-like median patch score; 13 do not. Source-frame inspection of [five changed windows](candidate-difference-contact.jpg) shows three new red cutaway candidates (T031, T064, T091) with **no** stone-like patch support. New yellow T083 has patch support but sits on a continuing on-ice sequence, so it needs a continuity check. The old T032 candidate that disappeared from the augmented ledger also has some stone-like patch tracks in a targeted check, illustrating that patch evidence alone is not a safe acceptance rule. No candidates were deleted by this audit.

The [joined visual-only cue table](visual-only-evidence-patterns.json) finds three with both stone-like appearance and unique colour-compatible held-release passage, 12 with appearance only, two with release only, and 11 with neither. T031, T064 and T091 have neither; T083 has appearance only. The table keeps all proposals, since the missing cues cannot establish that no throw occurred.

The [counter-gap consistency audit](visual-only-counter-gap-screen.json) adds a distinct source signal. T031 and T091 are red proposals between continuous counter states with no red decrement. T064 overlaps red B52's counter-evidence interval, making duplicate interpretation plausible. T083 sits across a state gap that could hide a yellow decrement. These are contradiction/compatibility statuses, not physical throw labels, and the [multiscale handoff](multiscale-event-handoff.json) keeps every proposal with all three cue types.
