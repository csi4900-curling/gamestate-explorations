# Phase-aware non-origin fallback, retrospective correction

The first [non-origin continuity fallback](NONORIGIN-CONTINUITY.md) admitted three questionable Switzerland candidates because it used the origin model's probability while ignoring its separate `phase_supported` flag. That flag was **false** for `S322`, `S404`, and `S566`; the original origin router had already excluded them from delivery-view use. Norway `S465` and Swiss `S171` had phase support. This is a source-only pipeline inconsistency, discovered after viewing the first-transfer results.

The v2 rule keeps the frozen v1 continuity logic but requires explicit phase support before considering an origin-negative shot. The reproducible [retrospective audit](nonorigin-phase-v2-development.json) leaves one Norway end-seven candidate, zero end-eight candidates, and zero Slovakia candidates. In Switzerland it reduces four candidates to one, retaining red `S171` and removing `S322`, `S404`, and `S566`. The v1 artifacts remain unchanged for provenance.

This correction is **postscore development**, not a clean transfer or measured precision gain. Phase support itself may reject real throws, and none of these four cases has exhaustive independent physical event labels. The [camera-relative audit](CAMERA-RELATIVE-OBJECT-MOTION.md) adds a different source of evidence: `S404` has stone-like patches but negligible motion relative to the registered background, while `S322` remains unscorable because registration fails. The two checks should remain separate and explicit; neither justifies silently accepting or rejecting a throw.

## First full China transfer of the phase-aware rule

The unchanged v2 rule and both full-match source-only inputs were hashed in a [pre-run plan](china-nonorigin-phase-v2-first-plan.json) before inference on all **630** Canada–China camera shots. The [first output](china-nonorigin-phase-v2-first.json) contains only two red candidates, `S123` and `S519`. China had been exposed for other tasks, but it did not set this fallback's rule. The reserved independent source was not used.

A separate [post-transfer overlap check](china-nonorigin-phase-v2-overlap-postscore.json) finds **zero novel camera-shot windows**: both shots already have candidates in the existing China event ledger. `S123` is a red fallback cue in a window whose linked delayed counter decrements **yellow**; the ledger already flags that candidate's colour as conflicted. `S519` already has a red visual-only candidate without a counter link. The visual frames suggest why colour-only continuity is fragile, but neither the counter nor our frame review independently proves the delivered stone's identity. This first transfer supplies no demonstrated recall gain and one clear proxy-colour contradiction, so v2 remains unpromoted. It is not physical throw-event precision, recall or F1.

The source frames around `S322` and `S404` also contain changes near 3996.6 s and 5186.6 s that fall below the current conservative hard-cut threshold. Those changes occur after the principal cap transits, so they do not explain the apparent moving stone in `S404`; the camera pan does. No cut-detector threshold was changed from this selected inspection. The reserved independent source was not used.

## Selected China track-continuity and internal-edit diagnostic

A [camera-relative check](china-nonorigin-camera-relative-postscore.json) does not resolve the two China cues: the leading `S123` track moves relative to registered background features, while the leading `S519` track has no reliable registration pair. Camera-relative displacement alone does not certify a stone identity.

The [track-continuity audit](china-nonorigin-track-continuity-postscore.json) exposes a more specific `S123` association problem. Its 15-point leading transit moves 65.05 px in total, but one 63.05 px step from 1603.0 to 1603.4 s contributes **96.9%** of that path. Only one step exceeds 5 px. Two other long cap tracks in the same interval are almost stationary. A permissible nearest-neighbour jump between accepted cap-like patches can therefore satisfy the transit's displacement and path-ratio rules without showing sustained object movement. This is a diagnostic interpretation, not an independently labelled false-positive verdict.

At five samples per second, the strongest internal frame changes are at **1606.0 s** in `S123` (mean greyscale change 61.893, histogram distance 0.277) and **7029.4 s** in `S519` (74.874, 0.3106). Both fail the frozen hard-cut rule because it requires histogram distance at least 0.35, so the existing shot manifest spans possible edits. The first change occurs after the leading `S123` transit and cannot explain its earlier jump. Neither selected peak is proof of a general cut-detector recall problem; a full source-separated cut study is needed before changing thresholds or event counts.

These checks were selected after the China fallback transfer and overlap audit. They use source frames and the frozen cap model, not official shot labels or counter colours. The first-transfer and event-ledger artifacts remain unchanged. A next rule should require motion distributed across several observations and test it against independent source windows before promotion, while preserving abstention when camera registration or track linkage fails.

The [cross-recording track-shape audit](nonorigin-track-continuity-development.json) applies the same measure to the five earlier selected Norway/Swiss fallback windows. The potentially relevant `S465` and `S171` tracks have five and four steps over 5 px, with their largest steps accounting for 26.4% and 39.7% of path length. The camera-pan case `S404` also has distributed raw image motion: 11 such steps and an 11.9% largest-step share, although all six reliable one-second background registrations place its maximum camera-relative residual below 10 px (7.297 px). Thus a distributed-step criterion would reject the China `S123` association jump but **would not reject the Swiss pan**. `S322` and `S566` have no reliable camera pairs, so a combined check must abstain rather than treat them as negatives. These are selected postscore comparisons, not a calibrated rule or physical-event accuracy estimate.
