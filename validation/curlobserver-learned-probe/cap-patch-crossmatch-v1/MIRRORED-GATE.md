# Mirrored early-ice gate transfer across Norway ends

This is a development study on the exposed Canada–Norway 2022 broadcast. It tests the suggestion to detect a throw when a stone crosses an early visual line in the delivery view. The cue is useful, but the first transfers below show that a fixed line and a simple side router do not yet provide robust throw segmentation or physical event F1.

On exposed end four, a right-side gate at x ≥ 400, y ≥ 300 in a 640 × 360 frame, with frozen broadcast cap-patch score ≥ -0.6, supplied a provisional colour in all 17 origin-positive shots. Its [postscore development comparison](norway-end4-departure-gate-counter-proxy-development.json) paired 12 candidates with all 12 clean delayed counters, all with matching colour. The line and threshold were chosen after viewing known end-four failures, so this is not validation. Five candidates were unpaired, and four of 16 official shots lacked clean counter anchors.

The **first unchanged right-gate transfer** to end five [failed](norway-end5-departure-gate-counter-proxy-first.json): 13 of 15 origin-positive shots had no qualifying cap; only two candidates were emitted, and the sole counter pair had conflicting colour. Source frames show the delivery view is mirrored: stones travel toward the left side. A postscore [left-side diagnostic](norway-end5-left-gate-development.json), x ≤ 320 and y ≥ 280 with the same cap score, recovered 14 coloured candidates and retrospectively paired ten with all ten clean end-five counters with matching colour. This geometry was selected after the failed first transfer.

Using the exposed end-four and end-five gate observations, a [source-only orientation router](norway-end4-end5-dual-gate-development.json) was then fixed. It lets coherent cap motion select left or right and carries that choice through shots with weak evidence. A side is never selected from counter colour or official shot order. The [first end-six dual-gate scan](norway-end6-dual-gate-first.json) covered all 20 frozen origin-positive camera shots. Its [source-only routed decisions](norway-end6-dual-gate-events-first.json) emitted 19 coloured candidates. In the [delayed-counter comparison](norway-end6-dual-gate-counter-proxy-first.json), 12 paired with all 12 clean updates, but only ten paired colours agreed and two conflicted; seven visual candidates were unpaired. The official end has 16 deliveries and four without clean counter anchors. Timing agreement with selected counters is not physical throw precision or recall.

The router did switch from left to right during the end-six partition, as the source views require. Two colour conflicts (`S391`, `S417`) expose a separate failure: source frames show yellow stones held near the player before the broadcast cut, while the fixed right line sees too little trustworthy yellow evidence. Later red non-stone image content becomes a motion cue. A line placed farther into the visible ice can be missed when the camera cuts early; a line placed nearer the held stone risks counting preparation as a throw. The next detector needs a held-stone and motion-onset state, layout-aware ice geometry, and explicit cross-cut identity uncertainty. A receiving-view stone moving after impact cannot alone determine which colour was delivered.

A [held-origin development and first-transfer audit](HELD-ORIGIN.md) followed. It corrected both known end-six colour conflicts retrospectively, but its frozen rule made no overrides on the first end-seven scan. End seven paired 10 of 12 clean delayed counters with matching colour for each pair, leaving two counter-backed events missed. This is a same-match counter proxy, not physical event F1.

The [earlier adjacent-view study](NORWAY-ORIGIN-RECEIVER.md) and this study use developer-selected rules and delayed broadcast counters. None establishes the 0.95 full-recording precision and recall target. The reserved independent source was not used. Artifacts record source, model, rule and runner hashes so the first transfers can be replayed and audited.
