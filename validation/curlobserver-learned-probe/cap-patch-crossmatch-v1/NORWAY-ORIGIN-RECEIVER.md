# Norway broadcast cap and adjacent-view event study

This is an exposed development study of the [Canada–Norway 2022 replay](../../throw-event-benchmark/paralympics-2022-canada-norway/RESULTS.md), not independent physical throw F1. The public stone-box model improved on public clips but failed to recognize several visible side-view stones. An older broadcast cap-patch verifier, trained on developer-reviewed China and Slovakia samples, accepted the visible yellow cap at source time 1399 s and rejected nearby red cap candidates. Its frozen model is `model-excluding-swiss-transfer.npz`.

In the [eight selected end-2 windows](norway-end2-targeted-transfer.json), a hard verified-cap version of the old three-second track rule produced **no** supported track, including agreement controls. Real stones often crossed the side view in under a second. A [short transit rule](norway-end2-short-transit.json) then linked accepted patches within one camera shot, requiring at least three samples, 0.4 s and 30 image pixels of smooth displacement. Its top cue had the delayed counter's colour in six of eight selected windows; two controls had no cue. This rule was shaped on those windows and is not a test result.

We froze that cue and scanned **all** camera shots in the next end before comparing with counters. The [end-3 source-only scan](norway-end3-short-transit-first.json) found 71 local transits in 38 of 64 camera shots, versus 16 official deliveries. The [counter comparison](norway-end3-short-transit-counter-proxy.json) matched all 14 clean counter anchors only by allowing a large excess of visual cues. This is a useful recall-oriented component, not a precise event detector.

A source-only [origin/receiving-view pairing rule](norway-end3-origin-receiver-pairs.json) then created at most one provisional event per origin-positive shot. It prefers a cap colour seen in both the origin and immediately following camera shot; otherwise it keeps a single-view cue with its weaker evidence route. It retains the entire origin shot as a release window. End three yielded 17 candidates, 14 of which paired with all 14 clean counter updates with matching colour in the [development proxy comparison](norway-end3-origin-receiver-counter-proxy.json). Two of the 16 official end-3 shots have no clean counter anchor, and the delayed matcher can select the wrong visual cue.

The **first transfer** of the frozen visual rules to end four is the decisive check here. The [end-4 source-only scan](norway-end4-short-transit-first.json) covered 73 camera shots and found 53 transits in 35 shots. The [frozen pairing](norway-end4-origin-receiver-first.json) produced 15 provisional events from 17 origin-positive shots. Against 12 clean delayed counter anchors, the [counter-proxy comparison](norway-end4-origin-receiver-counter-proxy-first.json) paired 10 events: six had matching colour and four conflicted. Two clean counters and five visual candidates remained unpaired. The official end has 16 throws, so this cannot establish full-end recall. It does show that the end-3 pairing result did **not** transfer reliably even within the same match.

Three of the end-4 colour-conflict pairs used receiving-view evidence only; one used origin-view evidence only. Receiving views can show other stones moving after impact. A same-colour cue across a broadcast cut is also not independent proof of persistent physical identity. We need explicit object-level association, phase context and abstention on ambiguous colour; simply lowering the patch threshold, adding short tracks, or trusting a delayed counter assignment will not meet the 0.95 precision and recall gate.

All artifacts record source, model and code hashes. The reserved independent recording was not used. The origin classifier and cap verifier were frozen for these scans; end-3 short-transit and pairing rules were developed on exposed earlier windows, then transferred unchanged to end four. Every reported match is a **counter proxy**, never a validated physical release timestamp.
