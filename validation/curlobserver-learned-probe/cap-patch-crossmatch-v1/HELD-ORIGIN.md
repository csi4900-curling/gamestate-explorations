# Held-origin cue: development and first transfer

This study asks whether a cap near the delivery player, held before a broadcast cut or moving through the lower central ice, can correct the early-line colour decision. It does **not** establish the instant of release or a physical throw identity.

On exposed Norway end six, inspection of the two known colour conflicts (`S391`, `S417`) showed yellow delivery stones near the player while the early gate selected red image content. We selected a side-dependent central corridor and a permissive frozen broadcast cap-patch score, then measured stationary hold duration and short transit displacement. A rule chooses a colour only for a dominant hold of at least 3 seconds or dominant transit of at least 60 pixels. Otherwise it leaves the existing mirrored-gate decision alone. This was **postscore development**: the rule was designed after seeing the end-six errors. The [development scan](norway-end6-held-origin-development.json), [decisions](norway-end6-held-origin-events-development.json), and [counter comparison](norway-end6-held-origin-counter-proxy-development.json) show three overrides among 19 candidates. The two known colour conflicts were corrected; the delayed-counter proxy became 12/12 timing pairs with 12/12 matching colours. Seven candidates remained unpaired. These numbers cannot be used as independent validation.

We then froze the cue and decision code and scanned all 17 origin-positive shots in Norway end seven before looking at its counters. The [first source-only scan](norway-end7-held-origin-first.json) records the model, code, source, and prior-result hashes. The [source-only decisions](norway-end7-held-origin-events-first.json) made **zero overrides**: 14 coloured candidates, exactly the prior mirrored-gate result. The [first delayed-counter comparison](norway-end7-held-origin-counter-proxy-first.json) paired 10 of 12 clean updates; all 10 paired colours agreed. Four visual candidates were unpaired, two clean counter updates were not matched, and four of the 16 official deliveries had no clean counter anchor. The proxy candidate precision is 10/14 and proxy recall 10/12, but these are **not** physical event precision or recall. A monotone counter pairing may choose the wrong delivery, and end seven remains part of the same development match.

The transfer supports a narrow conclusion: the frozen held cue did not damage end-seven colour agreement, but it did not recover its missed events. The early virtual line and held cap are complementary image cues; neither yet supplies reliable one-stone identity across cuts. The next evaluation must use complete independent event labels, including deliveries without clean counters, before reporting throw precision, recall, or F1. The reserved independent source was not used here.
