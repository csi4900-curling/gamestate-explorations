Your first labelling session

You are recording visible evidence. You do not need to judge tactics, scores or whether a shot was good. Uncertainty is useful information.

1. Practise for two deliveries

Open the practice page →

  1. Click Load supplied video, then Go to practice start. This uses the existing Canada–Korea footage, not the reserved match.
  2. Watch from 28:40. Follow the sheet shown in the left departure view and its receiving view. Find the next two deliveries.
  3. Pause just before a player sends a stone down the sheet; mark delivery start. Continue until it has left the departure view, pause and mark delivery finish. Choose its handle colour. The interval should cover the visible departure, not just the release instant.
  4. Follow the receiving view. Watch several seconds after the last visible movement. Capture a board if it is clear, and click the centre of the visible stone body for each stone on this sheet. Use the handle colour to choose red/yellow. Avoid the top of the handle or the shadow.
  5. Leave completeness unchecked unless the whole in-play layout is accounted for, including stones in front of the rings. If the view is unusable, enter a reason and click Record board as unobservable.
  6. Click Save marked board after marking stones and reviewing completeness. Starting the next throw also saves the pending board. Then export practice-draft.json. You may reveal the exploratory reference after trying. Return the practice draft to the assistant for a consistency review before tackling a whole end.

Practice labels are kept separate and cannot serve as independent test labels. The reference is exploratory AI annotation; it is not an expert answer key.

2. Label the reserved match

Open independent review →

  1. Load the supplied video. Enter your name and disclose any prior use of this recording for model tuning. Confirm you are labelling without predictions.
  2. Find the first fully recorded end after introductions/practice. An end is a round of deliveries toward one target house. Confirm which sheet/match the panels show. If the recording begins mid-end, explain this in notes and choose the next complete end. If you cannot tell, export a draft with the candidate times and ask for review.
  3. Set the start before its first delivery and the finish after its final outcome, before the next end begins. Write the sheet/panel description in setup notes. Do not assume the left panel always means departure.
  4. Work through all deliveries in order. Add one board record for each, even if the board is unobservable. Do not force a particular throw count or alternating colours. After watching the whole selected end, tick the full-end review box only if every delivery, including uncertain ones, has a record.
  5. Use Check draft to find missing records. Export reference-draft.json at the end of each session. Import it to continue on another browser/computer. A browser backup also preserves your latest work on the same page/browser.

When to stop guessing

  • Someone blocks the stone: note the obstruction; do not invent a centre or identity.
  • A stone vanishes: disappearance alone does not mean it left play.
  • A collision moves several stones: wait for all visible movement to stop. Hidden motion remains unknown.
  • You see only the rings: there may be other stones in front of them. Keep completeness unchecked.
  • A player moves stones by hand, repeats a delivery, or officials intervene: record the times and uncertainty. Request experienced review instead of deciding the rule.
  • Two stones have the same colour: that does not establish identity across cameras. Leave identity unknown.

Unknown completeness reduces how much we can evaluate, but is much better than a confidently wrong label. A curling-knowledgeable reviewer should check ambiguous cases and a sample of clear ones before sealing.

No terminal commands or JSON editing are needed for the basic task. Give the exported file back to the assistant; sealing and scoring happen later. Exact release, cross-view identity and physical measurements are optional specialist follow-up tasks.
