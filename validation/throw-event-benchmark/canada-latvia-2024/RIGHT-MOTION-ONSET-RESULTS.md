# Latvia right-view observed motion boundaries

The full Latvia area-aware tracks frequently contain a long near-stationary colour association before rapid image travel. Treating the track's first frame as a throw start would be wrong. The [postreview development plan](RIGHT-MOTION-ONSET-PLAN.json) fixes a narrower cue: at least three 1 Hz steps with ≤3 px/s absolute vertical motion, followed within five seconds by two consecutive steps of ≥12 px/s in the proposal's direction. The [implementation](../../../prototype/right_motion_onset.py) returns a bracket from the last supported quiet frame to the first strong step. It does **not** infer physical release or classify a stone.

The [full 100-proposal segment ledger](right-observed-motion-segments-development.json) keeps cross-view episode IDs, source-visible class where reviewed, a short observed moving pixel path, and the prior partial-image-state link. **Forty-eight** tracks have a supported quiet-to-motion transition, **50** lack enough observations, and **two** have no supported transition. In the 20 end-six right-only proposals that received source-frame review, all **16 moving-stone-pixel paths** have a transition, but so does **one of four person/clothing paths**. This source was used to choose the threshold, so these counts are in-sample diagnostics, not detector accuracy.

For the 16 stone-pixel paths, 11 onset brackets are one second wide and five are two seconds wide at the 1 Hz sampling rate. The stored moving portion after the first strong step lasts only **one to two seconds**. The bracket means only that directed pixel travel became observable in that interval; the stone might have been released earlier, and a track can switch between same-colour objects. The last saved moving point is not the end of the throw. Physical release, camera-to-camera identity, settled board and simulation-ready metric trajectory remain unknown on every row.

This timing cue is therefore useful for **candidate video segmentation and source review**, especially in the formerly blind end-six interval. It cannot replace the appearance/context evidence, and it must not hard-reject short moving paths that begin after the object was already in motion. A different recording with source-reviewed onset intervals is needed to evaluate this cue's timing transfer; independently labelled full intervals are still needed for physical throw F1.

An unchanged [Canada–USA diagnostic](../canada-usa-2024/right-area-aware-observed-onset-latvia-rule-diagnostic.json) applies the same cue to 25 previously source-reviewed right proposals. It finds onsets on the three long paths with stone pixels but uncertain physical identity, and on one person/wheelchair path. All 17 reviewed short moving-stone paths remain **insufficient observations** for a quiet-to-motion transition. This is a cross-recording behavior check, not a timing-accuracy score: the USA source classes are same-agent reviews and supply no independently marked release/onset intervals.
