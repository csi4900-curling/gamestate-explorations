# Latvia full-right review queue (development)

The [reproducible queue](full-right-variant-review-queue-development.json) combines four proposal inventories from the same 7,058-second source: 100 one-second greedy, 118 two-second greedy, 114 two-second global first episodes, and four later global episodes. Its 336 **proposal records** are organized into 118 review groups. Each record keeps a variant-qualified track and episode ID, so a group does not delete or merge any source proposal.

Groups require matching colour claim and image-motion direction, with at most eight seconds between their first and last passage times. There are 96 groups represented by three variants, 11 by two, and 11 by one; six groups contain multiple proposals from the same variant. These are convenient source-review windows, **not 118 detected physical throws**. Multiple records in one group can represent the same path, separate objects, or a person and stone in the same time window. The grouping cannot establish stone identity or reject false proposals.

All 19 same-agent, source-reviewed visible-stone path witnesses retain at least one time/colour/direction match in the queue, including the previously missed R008 and A452 windows. This is a targeted diagnostic on paths already found while reviewing proposals, not exhaustive throw recall. The full match still lacks independent blind labels, so precision, recall and F1 for physical throw events remain unknown. Development on this match also means these counts cannot be presented as cross-video generalization.

The [source-pixel audit of all 11 single-variant groups](full-right-variant-singleton-source-audit.json) finds five person/equipment prefixes and six mixed or identity-uncertain prefixes. Four previously unaudited global-only windows have a [new source packet](full-right-global-singleton-source-packet/manifest.json): A083 and A217 follow person/wheelchair pixels; A497 and A530 change between person/equipment and stone pixels. The other seven use existing source packets. Because the mixed groups include stone pixels, **single-variant support is not a safe automatic rejection rule**. These are postproposal source classes, not independent throw labels.

The [audit of all 11 two-variant groups](full-right-two-variant-source-audit.json) combines earlier source reviews with a [new packet for the 11 previously unreviewed counterparts](full-right-two-variant-counterpart-source-packet/manifest.json). Three groups show stone pixels in both members, one shows stone pixels in one member and a mixed path in the other, four show person/equipment pixels in both, two show person/equipment plus a mixed path, and one is mixed in both. Thus **agreement between two association variants is not a reliable stone or throw label either**. The A323 global path is a useful example: its sampled prefix follows a red stone, while its nearby A312 greedy counterpart has mixed-object pixels. The pair's shared time and colour do not verify that the tracks represent one physical stone.

An unchanged [context-cue transfer](full-right-reviewed-context-transfer-development.json) scores all 33 reviewed proposal prefixes with the existing 0.25 surrounding-ring **and** below-point ice rule. It assigns the clear-context review tier to all seven stone-pixel prefixes (from four time groups), while sending 14 of 15 person/equipment prefixes to cluttered review. Two of 11 mixed/identity-uncertain prefixes also score clear; one person/equipment prefix scores clear. The older 0.44 ring-only rule leaves seven of 15 person/equipment prefixes clear. This makes the paired cue useful for review priority, but **neither tier is an automatic physical-throw decision**: the set is selected after proposal review, its paths are correlated within groups, and the clear mixed paths may splice a stone to another object. The rule's thresholds were fixed on earlier development recordings before this Latvia transfer.

The same unchanged cue now scores the [entire 336-proposal queue](full-right-all-proposal-context-review-development.json), reproducing all 33 reviewed measurements exactly. It assigns 267 proposals to clear-context review and 69 to cluttered review; at group level, 92 of 118 groups contain at least one clear proposal and 26 have only cluttered proposals. **All 336 proposal IDs remain present.** These are workload priorities, not a filtered event count: most high-consensus groups have no source-pixel class yet, and clear ice can surround a handled stone or a false track.

A fixed [high-consensus source probe](full-right-high-consensus-context-source-probe/manifest.json) inspected one proposal in each of the ten three-variant, all-cluttered groups and one proposal in ten clear groups spaced across the match. The [source-pixel audit](full-right-high-consensus-context-source-audit.json) finds **moving stone-body pixels in two all-cluttered groups**, R0067 and R0109. Their ring fractions are 0.2649 and 0.3268, respectively, above the frozen 0.25 boundary; both have low below-point fractions. Among the ten selected clear groups, nine sampled prefixes show stone pixels and one, R0117, follows a person despite a ring fraction of 0.0482 and below-point fraction of 0.2454. Of the ten all-cluttered sampled prefixes, seven follow people/equipment and one is mixed. This directly falsifies using the paired context cue as a hard reject or confirm gate on this match. The probe reviewed only one member per group and does not establish physical throw truth or event F1.

The previously frozen [side-view patch model](../../curlobserver-learned-probe/sideview-patch-transfer-v1/model.npz) was transferred unchanged to the same [53 source-reviewed proposal prefixes](full-right-reviewed-frozen-sideview-patch-transfer-development.json). At least one of three sampled boxes scores positive on eight of 18 stone-pixel prefixes, six of 23 person/equipment prefixes, and eight of 12 mixed prefixes. It does catch the two cluttered stone counterexamples R0067 and R0109, and rejects the clear person counterexample R0117. But **10 stone-pixel prefixes score negative in all three samples**, so patch negativity cannot reject a candidate. Patch positivity also cannot prove delivery: false person/equipment patches and handled stones remain possible. The reviewed paths are selected and correlated, so these are source-pixel transfer counts, not independent object or throw-event performance estimates.

The [lossless full-match cue-pair handoff](full-right-all-proposal-visual-cue-pair-development.json) applies those two **unchanged** measurements to every proposal. Of 336 proposals, 118 have both visual cues positive, 172 have disagreeing cues, and 46 have both weak. Of 118 review groups, 41 contain a dual-positive proposal, 63 contain disagreement but no dual-positive proposal, and 14 have only weak proposals. On the 53 selected source-reviewed prefixes, the dual-positive tier contains six stone-pixel and two mixed paths but no sampled person/equipment path; the both-weak tier contains 15 person/equipment and four mixed paths but no sampled stone path. The 12 remaining stone-pixel prefixes lie in the disagreement tier. **All proposals and mixed paths remain in the handoff.** These counts prioritize review and do not establish a stone classifier or event filter; the selected reviews are too narrow and correlated to justify accepting dual-positive events or discarding weak ones.

A [frozen Estonia/Italy transfer](../RIGHT-VISUAL-CUE-CROSS-MATCH-TRANSFER.md) finds all 28 reviewed moving-stone prefixes in those two matches dual-positive, versus six of 18 selected Latvia stone-pixel prefixes. The source sets have different selection rules and correlated proposals, so this is not a comparable accuracy estimate. It is a concrete warning that the apparent strength of this visual filter on one match does not carry over unchanged to Latvia.

Run `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/export_latvia_right_variant_review_queue.py` from the repository root to regenerate the queue. The exporter checks the source video hash, identical 0–7,058 s scan intervals, global-scan lineage for the four later episodes, proposal counts, and witness source hash. It records hashes for each input and both code files. Unit checks are in `prototype/tests/test_right_variant_review_queue.py`. To reproduce the new source review, run `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/build_latvia_global_singleton_source_packet.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/audit_latvia_right_queue_singletons.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/build_latvia_two_variant_counterpart_packet.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/audit_latvia_two_variant_groups.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/score_latvia_reviewed_right_context.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/score_latvia_full_right_review_queue.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/build_latvia_high_consensus_context_probe.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/audit_latvia_high_consensus_context_probe.py`, `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/score_latvia_reviewed_sideview_patches.py`, and `feasibility/scripts/python.sh prototype/scripts/score_latvia_full_right_visual_cue_pair.py` in that order.

All 22 groups supported by fewer than three variants now have source-pixel review, and all 118 groups have paired visual-cue priorities. Twenty of the 96 higher-consensus groups have a fixed one-prefix source probe. The next detector step is improved stone-body/person evidence that can handle nearby people and painted ice, with abstention on occluded or spliced tracks and transfer checks across other matches. Neither frozen context nor frozen patch evidence is safe as a hard gate. A separate independently labelled full-match timeline is still needed to measure missed throws and false events. Release and settle times, complete post-throw boards, metric paths, spin, and simulation-ready states remain unresolved.
