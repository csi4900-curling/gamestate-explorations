# Full Canada–Latvia right-camera area-aware transfer

This is a **prediction-only full-recording transfer**, not a measured throw-event F1. The [prerun plan](FULL-RIGHT-AREA-TRANSFER-PLAN.json) froze the Canada–USA-developed area-aware right-colour tracker, late-seed span rule, source hash, and 0–7058 s interval before this scan. Latvia had been exposed in other detector work, so it is a transfer of unchanged code rather than a wholly unseen recording or independent physical-throw evaluation.

The [full right scan](full-right-area-first-transfer.json) found **485 raw colour tracks** and **100 directional review proposals**. The [cross-view inventory](full-right-area-episodes-first-transfer.json) joins them to all **119** earlier left triggers (72 provisional accepts, 47 withholds) without changing either decision. It preserves every left and right ID in **147 review episodes**: 62 one-to-one temporal candidates, 49 left triggers without a compatible right proposal, 32 right-only proposals, two shared-one-right groups, and two multiple-right groups. Among the 72 provisionally accepted left triggers, 61 have a one-to-one right match, one is ambiguous, and ten have no compatible right match. Camera-order, colour, and time agreement are not physical stone identity.

## End-six source check

The displayed end-six interval at 5670–6670 s had a static or blind left delivery view in the previous [coverage audit](../layout-router/full-2024-coverage/LEFT-BLIND-ICE-ACTIVITY-RESULTS.md). The earlier [dense right lower-ice probe](../layout-router/full-2024-coverage/LATVIA-END6-DENSE-RIGHT-PROBE.md) found clothing on its surviving upward paths and no right-only entry candidate. The new tracker finds **20 right-only proposals** in that interval. All 20 were rendered before context scoring in five [marked source sheets](full-right-area-end6-right-only-source-review/manifest.json), then assigned [same-agent source-frame classes](full-right-area-end6-right-only-source-review/source-prefix-classes.json): **16 show moving stone pixels** at the marked path, and **four** (R01:A422, A424, A427, A431) follow people or clothing. The 16 visually stone-like proposals comprise eight red and eight yellow. This visual pattern is useful candidate evidence; it is **not** an independently verified count of 16 physical deliveries. Some tracks stay near-stationary for many seconds before moving, and three sampled frames cannot prove uninterrupted same-stone identity or exact release time.

The previously developed ring-plus-underpoint context rule was then applied unchanged to **all 100** right proposals under a separate [pre-score plan](FULL-RIGHT-CONTEXT-PAIR-PLAN.json). The [score artifact](full-right-area-context-pair-first-transfer.json) puts 15 of the 16 reviewed stone-pixel paths in clear context and all four person paths in cluttered context. **R01:A444 is a visible moving yellow stone but scores cluttered**: the surrounding non-ice fractions at its three samples are 0.3855, 0.3268 and 0.0143. The first two frames include foreground material near the stone; the underpoint band remains clear. A hard context rejection would therefore lose at least this visibly stone-like proposal in the reviewed set. The source classes were made by the detector developer after seeing proposals, and neither class is an independent throw label.

## Partial state handoff

The prior [periodic right-house scan](../layout-router/full-2024-coverage/LATVIA-END6-PERIODIC-BOARD.md) supplies time-indexed, camera-local stable-cap observations. A [bounded handoff](full-right-area-end6-partial-state-development.json) searches 15–35 s after each of the 16 stone-pixel proposals, stopping five seconds before the next right-only proposal. **Thirteen** find a following partial image observation; **three** find none. The four person-path proposals are not given event-state observations. Most linked windows contain just one stable cap. These are neither complete settled boards nor verified before/after transitions; all physical-throw, settle, same-stone and simulation-ready fields remain false or unknown. The follow-up window was selected after inspecting this source, so its coverage is development evidence.

The full scan substantially improves **review coverage** in a previously blind camera interval, and the new inventory connects candidate motion to existing partial board observations without fabricating a complete game state. The remaining gates are independent complete-interval throw labels for event precision/recall/F1, temporal stone identity through the camera views, and validated complete metric boards before simulation or coaching claims.

A later [observed-motion boundary ledger](RIGHT-MOTION-ONSET-RESULTS.md) brackets the visible transition from held/quiet colour pixels to sustained movement for all 16 reviewed stone paths at 1–2 s resolution. One reviewed clothing path also has that transition. The cue helps segment source video for review but does not infer a physical release or throw completion.

A [1080p body/handle correspondence check](END6-CROSS-RESOLUTION-BODY-RESULTS.md) finds a unique synchronous high-resolution path for each of those 16 source-reviewed moving-stone paths. It improves observed-frame body localization but extends the moving observation by at most one second; the full trajectory and settled state remain unavailable.

The subsequent [camera-boundary and censored-state audit](END6-FIELD-OF-VIEW-RESULTS.md) finds provisional next-second colour continuations for 15 paths and joins them to 13 later partial image boards without claiming a physical settle or same-stone board transition. A selected source sequence shows one stone reaching the bottom of the right camera after the earlier search window dropped it.

The [full-match candidate union](FULL-MATCH-AREA-CANDIDATE-UNION.md) places all 20 later right-only source reviews beside the earlier left and right proposals. Its 96 possible-event review rows equal the nominal six-end shot count by coincidence, with known false and unverified candidates still present; it is not event-accuracy validation.

Two source-pixel audits test whether right-camera agreement can be promoted to an event rule. The [withheld-pair audit](WITHHELD-CROSS-VIEW-SOURCE-RESULTS.md) finds a uniquely paired person/wheelchair false corroboration, while the [accepted-no-right audit](ACCEPTED-NO-RIGHT-SOURCE-RESULTS.md) finds eight stone-shaped left paths lacking any compatible right proposal. Right-view timing agreement is useful for review, but neither a sufficient confirmation nor a safe hard requirement.
