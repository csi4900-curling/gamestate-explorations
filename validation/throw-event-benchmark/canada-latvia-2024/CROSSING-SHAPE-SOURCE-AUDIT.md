# Crossing shape is useful but insufficient (development audit)

The [reproducible audit](crossing-shape-source-audit-development.json) joins raw Latvia left-camera crossing measurements to three existing source-pixel reviews. It covers **24 distinct reviewed left crossings**: 12 marked pixels that visibly have a stone body, 9 that visibly belong to a person or wheelchair, and 3 ambiguous. These are same-agent, postproposal source reviews, **not independently labelled throw events**. Reproduce with `python prototype/scripts/audit_latvia_crossing_shape.py` from the project root; the JSON records the SHA-256 of each input and the runner.

The current `mean_body_support` is not a stone classifier by itself: accepted **S45**, whose marked pixel is a person's head, scores **0.977**, while stone-shaped **S59** also scores **0.977**. Looking at raw candidate box shape helps somewhat. The largest height/width ratio among the 12 reviewed stone pixels is **1.50**; the smallest body-support score is **0.923**. Using those *in-sample* boundaries as a diagnostic, a veto for `height/width > 1.50` or `body_support < 0.923` would flag **6 of 9** reviewed person-pixel crossings without flagging these 12 stone-pixel crossings. But **S72, S116, and S118** are still person pixels and pass both checks. There is no validated hard filter here.

The thresholds are fitted to this selected set. They say nothing yet about unreviewed Latvia candidates, another recording, or full-match event precision/recall. The practical implication is to retain these measurements as ranking features and test richer source-object and temporal evidence across recordings before changing accepted/withheld decisions. The [full-match candidate union](FULL-MATCH-AREA-CANDIDATE-UNION.md) remains a proposal inventory, not a verified 96-throw segmentation.

Adding the raw track duration does not remove the ambiguity. The longest reviewed stone-pixel candidate lasts **1.8 s** in the recent track window. A duration veto above that in-sample boundary additionally flags person-pixel **S72**, making **7 of 9** person pixels flagged by the three checks. **S116 and S118** still pass all three; their duration and observed span overlap reviewed stone-pixel paths. Direction consistency is also 1.0 for these two, as it is for most reviewed stones. A longer or monotone colour track is therefore not, by itself, proof of a stone or throw. This duration boundary is also selected from the same Latvia reviews and is not a transferred rule.

As a distribution check, the same Latvia-derived three-part veto would flag **35/109** accepted Italy proposals, **28/61** Estonia proposals, **37/131** China proposals, and **63/122** Scotland proposals in the saved raw runs. Durations are compared at millisecond precision to avoid treating equivalent 1.8-second values differently due to floating-point rounding. These are counts of proposals that would change decision, **not errors**: those other recordings do not have source labels for these rows in this audit. The sizeable fractions make a hard-filter change unjustified without source review and an independent throw-event evaluation.

The [exact-source Italy S03 review](../canada-italy/SHAPE-VETO-S03-SOURCE-RESULT.md) supplies one direct counterexample to using the Latvia boundary as a hard veto: the flagged candidate's marked pixels follow a yellow-capped stone body in motion. It still does not establish that S03 is a verified physical delivery, or estimate how many of the other flagged proposals are stones.
