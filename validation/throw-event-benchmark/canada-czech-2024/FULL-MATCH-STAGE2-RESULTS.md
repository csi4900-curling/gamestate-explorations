# Canada–Czechia 2024: full-match throw-candidate screen

**Result.** The fixed pipeline has now processed the complete 0–6683 s broadcast through candidate detection, high-resolution receiving-motion checks, camera registration, bounded-gap/dropout handling, stone-appearance screening, and shared-path deduplication. It retained **79 provisional candidates** from 147 raw motion candidates. These are **not 79 verified throws**. Source inspection identifies **two accepted false-positive paths (S96 and S143)**, so 77 accepted candidates remain unreviewed. There is no full-match F1 without exhaustive independent labels.

The [full-match evidence explorer](full-match-review/index.html) lets a reviewer jump from any raw candidate to the original broadcast and inspect the conditional path, post-event partial board, and observation tracklets. It is a prediction-visible review tool; independent labels must use the separate blind packet.

## Post-event board observations

The same view-local quiet-tail observer used on the frozen interval was run on all **79 accepted screen candidates**, including S143 so its false-positive behavior remains auditable. The [79-row board merge](full-match-partial-boards-postscore.json) passed all singleton-shard identity, configuration, and hash checks. It found 66 stable **partial** boards, nine tails with registered frames but no stable stones, and four candidates with no usable quiet tail. Across those tails, 1615 frames registered and 124 abstained. The [board-augmented 147-candidate timeline](full-match-evidence-with-boards-postscore.json) attaches these observations without claiming complete board state or simulation readiness.

The [adjacent-board ledger](full-match-adjacent-boards-postscore.json) gives 40 conditional same-view comparisons across 78 successive accepted-candidate pairs, with 132 spatially associated observation pairs. It abstains on 17 pairs lacking a partial board, 16 with intervening passage candidates, and five camera changes. A spatial association is not verified stone identity or a proven stone movement.

The [observation-tracklet handoff](full-match-observation-tracklets-postscore.json) now joins only those 132 supported pairs. It assigns 267 stone observations to **135 camera-local tracklets** across the 79 candidate board attempts. Tracklets stop at every abstained comparison or camera change; 84 contain only one observation, while the longest contains 12 successive observations. These are reusable partial-state identifiers for review, **not physical stone identities**. Every candidate state still has `complete_game_state: false` and `simulation_ready: false`.

On the 15 accepted candidates shared with the frozen interval, [board reconciliation](full-vs-interval-board-reconciliation-postscore.json) finds the same status category for all 15 and the same stone count for 14. S63 in the full-match scan has seven proposed stones, while its frozen-interval counterpart S22 has nine; two yellow proposals near the button are missing from the full-match board. Tail bounds differ for S59/S18 and S63/S22. This is a concrete stability limit on using the current boards as game state.

**Known failure.** S143 crossed the early ROI at 6374.697 s, after the sixth displayed marker had ended. The [source review](source-review/s143-review.json) and [frames at 6374.7 s](source-review/s143-6374.7.jpg), [6380.5 s](source-review/s143-6380.5.jpg), and [6383.0 s](source-review/s143-6383.0.jpg) show the final 4–13 display and players in wheelchairs leaving the ice. The motion tracker followed postgame movement; one of three path patches narrowly passed the learned appearance test. This is an agent source review, **not independent validation**. The frozen screen remains unchanged and its 79-candidate output still includes S143; the evidence timeline records the rejection separately.

A [source-only 5-second contact sheet](source-review/final-transition-6300-6400-5s.jpg) and [terminal-phase development record](source-review/terminal-phase-development.json) put the late raw-candidate cluster S141–S147 (6358.511–6396.912 s) in the visible stone-clearing and player-exit period. Stones are still distributed around the house at 6325 s; clearing is underway by 6335–6350 s; the 4–13 final display and departing players are visible by 6370 s. These observations do not timestamp the last throw or independently label all seven candidates. They identify a concrete hard-negative interval for testing a terminal-phase cue across recordings; no timestamp or displayed-end cutoff has been added to the detector.

The [three-recording terminal-scoreboard audit](../TERMINAL-SCOREBOARD-TRANSFER.md) shows why an unplayed-end “X” mark is insufficient for that cue: Czechia's known false path precedes the visible X, and Latvia's X appears well after the rink is already empty. The mark can support a terminal-state hypothesis when present but cannot safely define its start.

Its [source review](source-review/s96-review.json) and [high-resolution path frames](accepted-low-score-source-review/s96-path-review.jpg) show the selected path following a wheelchair footrest or shoes during in-game stone handling. This agent review is **not** independent validation.

**Postscore development finding.** Left-view registration was nearly complete, but the original minimum-step and minimum-travel path requirements missed short, slowing visible stone paths. A separate [uniform short-path experiment](LEFT-SHORT-PATH-RESULTS.md) retained all 79 frozen candidates and proposed nine more on this exposed match after the unchanged appearance screen. Five other existing recording intervals yielded no added paths. That experiment does not revise the frozen result or establish an F1 improvement.

The [corridor-withheld audit](OFF-CORRIDOR-REVIEW.md) also processed all 31 excluded raw spans through the unchanged high-resolution motion and patch checks. Six had candidate-local paths; four passed the one-of-three patch rule, but three of those four shared the same timed path. Source frames show all four distinct paths tracking players or a wheelchair, including the two patch-positive paths. This supports keeping the present corridor against those observed failure modes. It does **not** prove the corridor has no false negatives, because path failure and sampled source stills cannot establish that a delivery was absent. A simple requirement for two accepted patches would remove S143 but also discard three source-visible stone paths [S07](accepted-single-patch-source-review/s07-path-review.jpg), [S21](accepted-single-patch-source-review/s21-path-review.jpg), and [S42](accepted-single-patch-source-review/s42-path-review.jpg). A higher score threshold would remove S143 but also reject the source-visible yellow stone [S69](accepted-low-score-source-review/s69-path-review.jpg).

A separate [neutral-gray stone-body development check](BODY-BAND-TRANSFER.md) abstains on S96 while preserving the other 78 frozen full-match acceptances and all 80 provisional acceptances on five other 2024 recording intervals. It does not remove S143, has no independently labelled false-negative denominator, and leaves the frozen screen unchanged.

A newer [object-overhead development check](OVERHEAD-DARKNESS-TRANSFER.md) measures dark pixels immediately above each frozen patch-positive cap box. On the exposed 79 full-match acceptances and 80 accepted candidates across five other 2024 intervals, it would abstain only on the two source-reviewed wheelchair paths S96 and S143. All 159 paths were measured, but the feature and threshold were chosen after viewing those two failures. This is a candidate precision lead, **not** validated precision or an adopted detector gate; it can fail on real stones beneath a nearby player or graphic, and it does not address missed throws.

A [trigger-free receiving-camera probe](GAP-RECALL-PROBE.md) scanned a 130-second portion of the 248-second **accepted-event** gap in displayed end 2. The gap is not raw-candidate-free: the early ROI and corridor retained S40 (yellow, 1513.812 s) and S41 (red, 1590.967 s), then the frozen receiving-motion screen abstained on both. Source frames show corresponding yellow and red stone traversals. S40's path branches after a common prefix; the [S41 left-bound diagnostic](LEFT-BOUND-RECALL-DIAGNOSTIC.md) shows that the original −6.401 m proposal boundary clips the moving stone and that an isolated −18 m extension recovers an eight-sample, source-visible red-stone path. A [complete 32-candidate left-corridor check](FULL-LEFT-INTERVAL-BOUND-RESULTS.md) across five other recording intervals found two changed motion selections, Italy S03 and Scotland S02. In an [isolated patch audit](LEFT-BOUND-PATCH-RESULTS.md), the exact frozen appearance model supports all three source-visible paths, but also supports the known S143 postgame false path. These are not independently scored event-level recall or precision gains. The left trigger-free scan selected no paths; the right selected a player's hat. The frozen candidate sequence remains unchanged.

A later [full-match replay of all 60 left-corridor candidates](FULL-LEFT-BOUND-MATCH-RESULTS.md) found **12** no-path → unique-path changes under the same wider bound, including S41. All 12 selected paths have a visible coloured stone at first, middle, and last reviewed source samples and pass the unchanged frozen appearance model in isolated scoring. They were all abstentions in the original 79-candidate screen. An integrated postscore replay exactly reproduced the 79 frozen acceptances, then added these 12 while rerunning the unchanged patch screen and shared-timed-path deduplication, yielding **91 provisional candidates** with no new timed-path duplicate pairs. This is substantial candidate-level recall evidence, not independent event scoring or 91 verified throws; the frozen full-match result remains 79.

The [91-candidate partial-state handoff](LEFT-BOUND-STATE-HANDOFF.md) runs the existing quiet-tail board observer on all 12 additions and retains the previous 79 board rows unchanged. It produces 78 stable partial boards across 91 attempts, plus a 147-raw-candidate evidence timeline and camera-local observation tracklets. These are not complete game states or simulation inputs.

**What remains.** Independent exhaustive throw labels are needed to measure precision, recall, and F1, including misses outside the corridor. The single-anchor result must be stress-tested against changing camera poses in other recordings. For the supervisor's requested game state after each throw, the partial boards need independent stone-level validation, cross-view identity, explicit treatment of candidate false positives and misses, and calibrated coordinate error before they can become complete states. Current paths and partial boards cannot yet drive a trustworthy full-rink physics replay or coaching judgment.

The [full-match evidence union](FULL-MATCH-EVIDENCE-UNION.md) now gives a single chronological handoff of all 147 raw trigger IDs, the 91 provisional screen candidates, six additional pre-split review windows, partial boards and conditional right-view links. It preserves evidence tiers and unknown physical state rather than promoting review windows to verified throws.
