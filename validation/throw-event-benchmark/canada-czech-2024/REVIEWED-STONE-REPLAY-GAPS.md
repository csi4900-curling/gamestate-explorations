# Reviewed-stone trajectory-to-board gaps

The [reproducible gap ledger](full-reviewed-stone-replay-gaps-development.json) extracts all **21 Czech episodes** with a source-reviewed moving-stone right prefix from the [lossless episode-state handoff](SOURCE-DIVERSE-EPISODE-STATE-HANDOFF.md). It preserves the provisional 1080p receiving-motion samples, exact-time 360p path-overlap evidence, camera-local partial-board observations, and explicit replay blockers. Every row remains `simulation_ready: false`.

The [split-panel registration audit](SPLIT-PANEL-REGISTRATION-AUDIT.md) checks camera-relative motion on the 17 episodes with provisional pixel paths in the full-resolution source. It is a positive-only development check, not an event accuracy measurement.

The [Latvia-developed high-resolution underpoint transfer](CZECH-HIGHRES-UNDERPOINT-TRANSFER.md) measures source-image context on 20 reviewed stone-pixel prefixes and 23 person/equipment prefixes. It supplies a useful appearance review tier, not a verified throw classifier.

Seven episodes have all three *kinds* of observation: a reviewed stone prefix, at least two exact-time matching receiving-camera image points, and a post-event partial board. Eleven have a stone prefix and partial board but no exact sampled path overlap; three have a stone prefix without a stable partial board. Four of the 21 also lack a usable left-candidate motion path. These are evidence tiers for work planning, not validated physical throws or complete game states.

Even the seven strongest cases have an unverified **moving-path-to-settled-board identity link**. In five, the partial board contains at least one local observation of the claimed colour. The last tracked moving point precedes the first such observation by **0.5–6.0 s**, and its conditional projected position lies **0.572–8.826 m** from the nearest same-colour board proposal. These distances describe unobserved motion and uncertain association; they are not tracking errors or evidence that the nearest stone is the delivered one. In E022 and E106, the partial board contains **no detected red stone**, even though a moving red prefix is visible earlier. A partial board can omit stones, so this is missing evidence, not proof of physical absence.

E019 now has a [dense original-resolution source packet](e019-settle-continuity-source-probe/manifest.json) and a [same-agent source audit](e019-settle-continuity-source-audit.json). Eight frames sampled between 686.0 and 688.0 s show the red stone approaching a yellow stone, touching/overlapping it around 686.5–687.0 s, then remaining near the first red board detection while yellow separates. The last stored moving-path center at 686.5 s is **13.312 image pixels** from the first board red center at 687.0 s. This supports a local red path-to-board continuity hypothesis through contact; it is stronger than selecting the nearest same-colour board stone. Collision overlap makes exact cap centers ambiguous, and neither independent persistent identity nor complete contact dynamics is established. The replay ledger now carries this source-audit link on E019 while keeping `settled_stone_identity_verified: false` and `simulation_ready: false`.

A further [10 Hz source-pixel measurement](e019-dense-colour-cap-probe/measurements.json) and [position plot](e019-dense-colour-cap-probe/cap-centroids.png) sample 21 original 1080p frames from 686.0–688.0 s in a fixed local crop. The red HSV mask has one substantial component in all 21 samples; its centroid moves roughly 27 image pixels toward the house by 686.5 s, about 12 more by 687.0 s, and remains near the red board detection thereafter. Yellow is stationary before contact, then moves away, but its mask splits into two components at 686.6, 686.7, 686.8 and 687.0 s. Those yellow frames have no assigned center in the plot. The red local centroid is within 0.5 image pixel of the stored motion/board red centers at four sampled anchors, though those stages use related source pixels. This is a local, post-inspection colour-cap probe rather than independently verified stone identity or a transferable collision tracker. It does not measure revolution count: a center trajectory records translation, not the handle's changing angle.

The [full reviewed-gap source-pixel inventory](full-reviewed-settle-gap-cap-inventory-development.json) applies the same colour masks at 2 Hz to **all 12** reviewed-stone episodes that have both a moving-path endpoint and a later same-colour partial-board observation. Ten of the 12 contain at least one sampled frame with multiple same-colour components in the crop; only E001 and E019 have a single component at every sampled time. This count diagnoses association difficulty, not tracking accuracy: the crop is built from the endpoints, may contain other stones, and 0.5-second gaps can conceal overlap or identity switches. Five further reviewed-stone episodes lack a same-colour partial-board observation, and four lack a moving path, so the inventory cannot silently count those as failures or successes.

[Original-frame contact sheets](settle-gap-source-contact-sheets/manifest.json) make four gaps inspectable. [E001](settle-gap-source-contact-sheets/e001-source-contact-sheet.jpg) shows a red stone traversing the crop from its last moving-path point at 108.0 s to the first red board detection at 112.0 s without a second red cap in sampled frames. This supports a same-agent local path-to-board continuation hypothesis, subject to unobserved motion between frames. [E011](settle-gap-source-contact-sheets/e011-source-contact-sheet.jpg) shows a red stone approach another red stone near 500.5 s and continue rightward toward the 505.0 s board detection; two red components are present early, and the contact makes physical identity ambiguous. [E080](settle-gap-source-contact-sheets/e080-source-contact-sheet.jpg) has two other red stones and a red player logo in the crop while the moving red stone proceeds toward a later red board detection. [E096](settle-gap-source-contact-sheets/e096-source-contact-sheet.jpg) shows a moving red stone whose position does not match the first red board detection at 4584.0 s. The sheets retain all frame-local components and source hashes. None is independent event or persistent-identity validation.

The [board-blind cap continuation probe](full-reviewed-stone-cap-continuations-development.json) starts from the last two observed moving-path samples and uses only subsequent image components, constant-pixel-velocity prediction, bounded displacement and an abstention for competing caps. It reaches the first same-colour board-observation time in **4 of 12** eligible gaps. Three terminal cap centers lie **0.221–1.095 image pixels** from a same-colour board-frame detection (E001, E019, E080). E096 reaches that time but is **446.093 pixels** from the only red board-frame detection, demonstrating that a first same-colour board observation can belong to an existing stone. Eight probes abstain before the first board-observation time, including E011 at a competing-cap split after contact. These are development diagnostics, not a success rate: the path can switch identities between half-second frames, and the first board observation is not necessarily the delivered stone.

E096 exposes a more useful late link. Its board scan first detects red local observation **V01** at 4584.0 s while the delivered red stone is visibly still moving. A second stable red local observation **V02** begins at 4591.0 s. The [extended board-blind cap path](e096-late-red-board-continuation-development.json) follows 17 half-second source frames from 4583.0–4591.0 s and ends **0.340 image pixels** from the new red board-frame detection, versus **290.203 pixels** from the older red detection in the same 4591.0 s frame. The [source contact sheet](e096-late-red-source-probe/late-red-contact-sheet.jpg) visibly supports the red stone's local travel. The [additive 21-episode handoff](full-reviewed-stone-continuation-handoff-development.json) records V02 as a *provisional* late board-link hypothesis and keeps `settled_stone_identity_verified: false` and `simulation_ready: false`. V02's first stable detection is not a verified physical stop instant.

The [all-case later-onset probe](full-reviewed-later-board-onset-probes-development.json) checks this principle across the **12** eligible gaps. Eight contain at least one same-colour board observation that begins after the first one. Six cannot be followed to a later onset because the board-blind path already abstained. The two surviving cases give opposite results: E096's cap is **0.340 image pixels** from its later V02 detection, while E080's continuing cap remains on its first matched red stone and is **343.927 pixels** from the later V01 detection. Thus “latest same-colour stone” is no safer as a general delivered-stone rule than “earliest same-colour stone.” The handoff carries every later-onset checkpoint and still promotes no physical identity.

The immediate reconstruction task is to extend a stone path from its last observed motion sample into the quiet-tail frames, then test whether a stable board observation can be linked without a camera cut, collision ambiguity, or competing same-colour stone. Where that chain breaks, the state must remain partial. A rendering or physics simulation can show observed segments and uncertainty, but cannot invent the missing path or use the nearest settled proposal as ground truth.
