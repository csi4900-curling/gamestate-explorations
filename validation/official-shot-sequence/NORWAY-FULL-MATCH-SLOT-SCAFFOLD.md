# Canada–Norway 2022: full-match shot-slot scaffold

The [offline 128-shot viewer](norway-state-handoff/index.html) and [unified evidence-state handoff](norway-full-match-evidence-state-handoff-first.json) now bring the official reference, candidate event links and source-video partial boards together. Forty-seven slots have a stable source-video house observation; 25 slots have a retrospective post-counter diagram comparison. All 128 have an official schematic diagram, but **zero** have verified video-derived physical simulation state. The viewer keeps the reference and video panels separate.

The [frozen slot ledger](norway-full-match-official-counter-slot-scaffold-first.json) joins the **128 shot-order slots** in the independent official results PDF to **100 observed one-stone decrements** in the broadcast graphic. All 100 decrement colours agree with their assigned official shot colour. The counter provides a delayed proxy for a delivery, not a physical release timestamp or proof of a tracked stone.

The linked visual-candidate ledger associates 99 of the 100 counter observations with candidate windows. Counter B46 has no linked candidate. The separate S86 moving-stone rescue is a **proposal** for the first end's missing shot 16, with no counter support. The other missing slots remain unfilled in this scaffold. None of these rows is marked simulation-ready.

This alignment follows the remaining-stone count: for a 16-shot end, `shot_number = 17 − (red_remaining_before + yellow_remaining_before)`. A rise in the combined remaining count starts the next counter group. On this source there are eight groups, matching the eight official ends; each assignment has a unique slot and the expected colour. This is source-specific evidence, not a general proof that graphic resets always identify end boundaries. Counterless candidates cannot safely be assigned by the midpoint between neighbouring counter timestamps: the fifth-end scoreboard is still visible around 5298 s, after that midpoint.

The independent official ledger supplies order, team and rotation **direction**, but no video timestamps or number of revolutions. A full game state requires a verified video throw identity and observed board transitions for each shot. The current partial board work does not establish that full state or a calibrated 3D replay. Independent event labels and video-to-official matching are still needed before reporting throw precision, recall or F1.

The companion [end-display audit](norway-counterless-candidate-end-display-v2.json) reads the source-video end label at three frames in each of the 42 counterless candidate camera shots. Repeated OCR readings agree on a displayed end for 31 candidates; 11 remain unknown or conflicting. Seven of the supported candidates display **5TH END**, including S375 and S379 near 5260–5310 s, the end-transition area where counter-timestamp midpoint assignment fails. These are broadcast-display observations, not automatic physical-end or official-shot assignments. The [first OCR pass](norway-counterless-candidate-end-display-first.json) retained only 23 supported candidates before handling frequent `S`/`5` and `B`/`8` glyph confusions; both versions and their runner hashes are frozen.

The [counter-gap inventory](norway-counter-gap-inventory-first.json) records every interval between adjacent counter-linked video candidates that contains a missing counter slot or a counterless candidate. It covers 25 of the 28 missing counter slots and 39 of the 42 counterless candidates in 22 intervals. Only **one** interval has exactly one missing slot and one candidate with matching colour and a repeated same-end display: S281 is a proposal for official shot E04-S12. A [six-frame source review](norway-e04-s12-source-contact-sheet.jpg) shows a Canadian player pushing a red-handled stone forward in the fourth end; by 3898.0 s the delivery stick is visibly separate. The [post-cut frames](norway-e04-s12-board-transition-source-frames.jpg) show red reaching the house, yellow leaving the visible house, and red stable on the right from 3918 to 3921 s. The [board-transition manifest](norway-e04-s12-board-transition-review-first.json) records those timestamps and the official PDF diagrams: E04-S11 shows one yellow house stone, E04-S12 one red house stone on the right. This supports a source-observed *partial house transition* and the proposed official-shot association. The comparison is retrospective and does not prove continuous stone identity across the cut, the complete sheet state, or independent event accuracy. The other intervals contain missing observations, extra candidates, unknown colours, or end-transition ambiguity. The three missing eighth-end slots after the final counter-linked anchor lie outside these bounded intervals. The first end's S86 rescue is a separate source-motion proposal, absent from the fused counterless-candidate list.

The existing video board detector also produces a [structured partial house state](norway-e04-s12-partial-house-state-first.json) directly from the 3912, 3915, 3918 and 3921 s source frames. At 3921 s it detects one red and no yellow stone in the visible house, with red at image-relative house coordinates `(0.6410, 0.3397)`; the detected red pixel position is identical at 3918 and 3921 s. The independently extracted PDF diagram places its red symbol at `(0.6417, 0.3500)`, a retrospective separation of **0.0103 house radii**. This is diagram agreement, not an independently calibrated centimetre error or a full game state. The detector itself labels delivery-side orientation uncertain in these frames, so the artifact preserves image-coordinate semantics and the source-reviewed top-arrival direction separately.
