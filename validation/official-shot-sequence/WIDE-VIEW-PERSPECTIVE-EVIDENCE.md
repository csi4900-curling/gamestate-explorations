# Wide-view evidence after the China adjacent-board miss

The first [Canada–China adjacent-board transfer](CHINA-ADJACENT-BOARD-FIRST-TRANSFER.md) emitted only one stable overhead stone after end-two shot 12, while the official post-shot diagram has three. The close overhead house nearly fills the 640×360 frame and leaves two red stones at or beyond the frame edge. A later camera view, at 1632–1644 s, is still before the next provisional event window begins at 1652.4 s. It shows a wide, strongly elliptical house.

We selected those four later frames **after inspecting the failed transfer**, then ran the frozen cap-patch verifier without using official diagram positions. The [source-only image-space audit](china-end2-wide-perspective-cap-evidence-development.json) finds one wide blue-ring component in every frame, with an ellipse near image centre (339, 280), axes about 107×368 px, and three stable cap-like objects: red near (126, 203), red near (494, 208), and yellow near (337, 312). Each appears in all four frames. The official diagram, consulted afterward, also contains two red and one yellow symbol. This colour-count agreement is encouraging but does not prove one-to-one physical identity.

The existing complete-house detector rejects this view because it expects a nearly circular overhead ring. The visible caps are **image-space observations only**. An ellipse fit alone does not recover a reliable planar homography, especially across perspective depth; these pixels are not house-radius or metric ice coordinates. We therefore did not add them to the nine-board state export or claim a complete video-derived board.

A separate [postscore affine-normalization check](china-end2-wide-affine-projection-diagnostic.json) tests the tempting shortcut of dividing pixel offsets by the fitted ellipse radii and trying both axis signs. Even the best of four sign choices matches only **one of three** official stone positions within the existing 0.25-house-radius tolerance; forced same-colour matching has mean error about **0.405 house radii**. The official positions are used here only to diagnose the shortcut, never to select an orientation or create a runtime video state. This rules out promoting the simple ellipse fit as a calibrated board transform.

### A source-only body-contact correction

Frame inspection exposed a second issue: the cap coordinates are above the ice plane. In this low-angle view, the grey stone bodies extend about 17–18 pixels below their coloured caps. The [body-bottom estimator](../../prototype/wide_view_ground_contact.py) uses neutral grey support below each cap to approximate an ice-contact pixel. It found stable contact estimates in all four selected wide frames: red near (125, 221) and (494, 226), yellow near (337, 330). The yellow stone also appears in the earlier overhead partial board, where it lies on the opposite side of the house centre. That source-only overlap establishes a longitudinal view reversal. A full 180° reversal suggests the lateral axis flips too, but the near-centre yellow stone cannot independently prove that lateral sign.

In a separate [postscore contact diagnostic](china-end2-wide-body-contact-diagnostic.json), simple ellipse normalization of those *body-contact* points matches **all three** official symbols within 0.25 house radii under the 180°-reversal hypothesis; the mean error is **0.143 house radii**. Keeping the other lateral orientation yields **two of three** matches. This is a substantial improvement over projecting elevated cap centres, but it is a selected development case, scored after inspecting the China failure. The grey lower edge is still an approximate contact point; the ellipse normalization is not a measured homography, and the lateral orientation remains only a source-based camera hypothesis. No wide-view coordinates were added to the runtime board export.

### Adjacent-shot development check

Using the same frozen cap verifier and body-bottom estimator, a [selected check of the preceding shot](china-end2-s11-wide-fusion-development.json) sampled four wide frames at 1603–1604.5 s, inside the shot-11 delayed-counter interval. It found two stable red body-contact tracks at approximately (125, 221) and (493, 226), supported by four and three frames. Cap-like player and rack patches in the same frames failed the body-contact check. The prior source-only overhead scan had already observed a central red stone from 1575–1589 s. Combining these non-overlapping source observations under the inherited 180° camera hypothesis yields three red positions, and **all three match** the official shot-11 diagram in the separate postscore comparison (mean error **0.128 house radii**). The original first China transfer remains **zero complete boards from five shots**; this later targeted fusion does not rewrite that result.

This is still development evidence. The S11 times were chosen after looking at frames, the close and wide observations are separated by about 14–25 seconds, and a delayed counter does not itself prove that no later delivery or impact occurred between them. The two views do not establish persistent physical stone identities. The fused result is not added to the exported board sequence and cannot seed a simulation.

### First other-recording contact transfer

The [source-only Swiss scan](swiss-end2-wide-contact-first-transfer.json) applied the frozen China cap verifier, grey-body estimator and ellipse detector to the same five Swiss end-two counter windows used by the earlier overhead scan, sampling its pre-existing offsets. It found no wide ellipse in shots 10 or 14; three wide frames but no stable body-contact track in shot 11; one spurious/partial ellipse frame and no track in shot 12; and three wide frames with **one** stable yellow contact in shot 13. The earlier overhead observation for shot 13 contains **two** yellow stones, both visible by eye in the wide frames. The frozen cap proposal misses one of them. This is a concrete cross-recording recall failure, not a complete-board gain.

The single projected wide contact matches an overhead yellow position within 0.25 house radii under **both** an unflipped and a 180°-flipped coordinate hypothesis (one of two same-colour positions in each case). The source evidence therefore cannot resolve camera orientation from that overlap. We did not choose the orientation using the official diagram or export a fused state. This transfer shows why the China result cannot justify a generic wide-view board path yet: cap detection and view orientation both need broader, source-only evidence.

### Blue-ring proposal revision and another recording

The missed Swiss yellow cap is visible against the blue ring. Its HSV contour passes the colour and size checks, but the original proposal rejects it because only **0.079** of its surrounding patch counts as white ice; **0.731** is blue. A [wide-view-only proposal](../../prototype/wide_view_cap_proposal.py) accepts source-detected blue-ring surroundings and keeps candidates within 2.2 fitted ellipse radii. The learned patch scores for that cap are about −0.62, −0.61 and −0.67 over the three frames, just below the original −0.6 cutoff. A **post-transfer** [Swiss v2 audit](swiss-end2-wide-blue-ring-v2-development.json) with a −0.7 cutoff recovers both yellow body-contact tracks in shot 13, with three-frame support each. It also emits one yellow track in shot 11, which has no overhead board in this window for source-only comparison. The first Swiss score remains unchanged.

Both shot-13 tracks now match the two earlier overhead yellow observations within 0.25 house radii, but **both** 0° and 180° sign choices match both stones because the same-colour layout is nearly centrally symmetric. More detections therefore do not, by themselves, solve view orientation or physical identity. The cap proposal and threshold were developed *after* the Swiss miss and are not a new held-out accuracy result.

### A rink-marking cue resolves these two handoffs

The centre of the Beijing 2022 Paralympic house contains an asymmetric three-colour emblem. The [button-logo orientation detector](../../prototype/button_logo_orientation.py) restricts itself to that small painted area and records two image signs: whether the red emblem fragment lies left or right of the button centre, and whether it lies above or below the blue fragment. It abstains if either colour has too little support or an axis separation is too small. The [selected source-only audit](button-logo-cross-view-orientation-development.json) finds both signs reversed in **all 12 of 12** overhead-to-wide frame pairs for China shot 12 and **all 12 of 12** pairs for Swiss shot 13. It therefore supplies a 180° relative orientation for these views without consulting stones or official diagrams. The same code abstains on the most occluded early wide frames inspected in the adjacent China and Swiss windows; this cue remains specific to broadcasts where the emblem is visible.

The [logo-oriented cross-view handoff](button-logo-oriented-cross-view-boards-development.json) then uses that source-only 180° sign to compare grey-body contact points with earlier overhead observations. China shot 12 has three wide points, one of which overlaps the overhead yellow stone by **0.079 house radii**; the provisional union retains two additional red wide points. Swiss shot 13 has two wide points, both overlapping its two overhead yellow points by **0.115** and **0.141 house radii**. This resolves the *image rotation* ambiguity in those selected cases. It does not verify persistent stone identity, unchanged board state across the time gap, metric calibration or completeness. Every provisional union is explicitly marked `simulation_ready: false` and stays outside the main board export.

A separate [first Norway end-eight coverage probe](../curlobserver-learned-probe/cap-patch-crossmatch-v1/norway-end8-logo-gate-coverage-first.json) tested whether the same cue could help route an *early throw gate*. The code and all 19 existing origin-shot intervals were frozen in a [pre-run plan](../curlobserver-learned-probe/cap-patch-crossmatch-v1/norway-end8-logo-gate-transfer-plan.json). At 20%, 50% and 80% of each interval, the existing complete overhead/wide house detectors accepted **zero of 57 sampled delivery-view frames**. Therefore no logo signature was available and no early-gate decision could be checked or changed. This is a detector-coverage result on an exposed match, not evidence that the emblem is absent from every frame. The board-view orientation result must not be presented as an improved throw-event detector.

Before testing another recording, the blue-ring code, model, threshold, five pre-existing shot anchors and offsets were frozen in the [Slovakia plan](slovakia-wide-blue-ring-first-plan.json). The [first Slovakia result](slovakia-wide-blue-ring-first-transfer.json) finds wide ellipses in only two isolated sampled frames across those five windows and **zero stable wide-contact tracks**. This is a camera-coverage limit for the preselected windows, not a stone-level false-negative rate. It provides no evidence that the revised path transfers to another recording. The emblem-orientation cue was developed later on the selected China and Swiss handoffs; it has not rescued those Slovakia coverage gaps. The reserved independent source remains untouched.

This selected diagnostic explains a concrete path to better coverage: detect wide perspective views, estimate stone contact points rather than cap centres, calibrate rink geometry from markings, resolve camera orientation from shared source-visible stones, then fuse with close overhead observations across cuts. That method must be tested on other recordings and against independent event and board labels before any simulation-ready claim. The reserved independent source was not used.
