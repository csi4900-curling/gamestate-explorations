# Norway 2022 transfer of the prior-cap area gate

The [one-step transfer artifact](norway-2022-cap-area-gate-transfer-development.json) applies the **0.5–2× previous-cap contour-area gate chosen after the China 2022 size failure** to another 640 × 360 recording, Canada–Norway 2022. It reconstructs all 169 frozen shot-scoped cap tracks exactly from source video and probes one next sample 0.5 s later. Nineteen probes reach a camera-shot boundary and abstain under either rule; 150 remain in-shot. Other colour, motion and selection thresholds are unchanged. The Norway source was exposed during earlier detector development, so this is a transfer check, not a held-out evaluation.

| Next-step outcome | Scaled Czech absolute area | China-developed prior-cap area |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unique geometric choice | 18 | 108 |
| No cap inside motion gate | 131 | 42 |
| Competing-cap abstention | 1 | 0 |
| Shot-boundary abstention | 19 | 19 |

Of the 108 relative-gate choices, 92 replace baseline no-cap abstentions and 16 remain unique under both rules. The relative gate also removes two baseline unique choices and one baseline competing choice. These counts are **candidate continuation decisions**, not physical-stone matches, event detections, precision or recall. The input catalog contains non-stone image tracks.

The [selected source-frame packet](norway-2022-cap-area-source-probe/manifest.json) clarifies three cases. [S28-Y01](norway-2022-cap-area-source-probe/s28-y01-source.jpg) visibly follows a moving yellow stone in an overhead shot; the next yellow cap measures 331.5 source pixels², close to the previous 335 pixels², yet exceeds the scaled Czech 77.8 pixels² maximum. The prior-cap rule admits this particular visible stone continuation. [S304-R01](norway-2022-cap-area-source-probe/s304-r01-source.jpg) follows red/yellow objects in the sideline stone rack, and [S321-R01](norway-2022-cap-area-source-probe/s321-r01-source.jpg) follows a person's face. Neither is a moving stone; the relative gate's abstention on those next frames is not a lost true stone. The original catalog nevertheless carried both as cap-track candidates. These are selected, same-agent visual judgments, not independent annotations of all 169 tracks.

The result supports replacing a fixed apparent-size limit with a past-observation scale check **inside a candidate tracker**. It does not validate the tracker as a throw detector: scene type, foreground people, sideline stones, paint and camera cuts still need explicit handling, and candidate identities must be reviewed across complete trajectories. The more permissive gate should remain developmental until an independently labelled match permits event and stone-identity scoring.

## Why a hard local ice gate is unsafe

The follow-up [all-seed source-pixel audit](norway-cap-track-ice-context-development.json) measured an existing non-ice ring cue at the last two points of **all 169** catalog tracks. An exploratory rule requiring both ring fractions below 0.30 would retain 72 of the 108 unique next-step choices and reject 36. It separates the three selected first-review cases: S28 passes; the sideline-rack S304 and face S321 fail. That appearance is misleading.

The [span-ranked source packet](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/manifest.json) froze four largest-span unique paths that fail this rule and four that pass before viewing their frames. All eight selected paths visibly follow stones. In particular, [S390-R01](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/s390-r01-source.jpg), [S604-R01](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/s604-r01-source.jpg) and [S327-R01](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/s327-r01-source.jpg) approach or cross the blue house ring; [S480-R01](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/s480-r01-source.jpg) traverses multicolour rink artwork. These are four direct false-rejection examples for the proposed hard gate. The four passing examples are also visible stones, but eight selected reviews cannot estimate full-catalog error rates or physical identity accuracy.

Consequently, local white-ice fraction can be retained as **context evidence** but must not veto a coherent cap path. A useful next discriminator needs to distinguish printed rink markings from foreground people and sideline objects, potentially through a calibrated play-surface region or independent stone-body evidence, then be tested without tuning on an independent recording. Neither relative apparent size nor local whiteness alone solves stone identity.

## Camera-relative motion coverage

The [full-track camera-relative audit](norway-full-cap-track-camera-relative-motion-development.json) exactly reconstructs all 169 frozen cap paths and applies the existing background similarity registration to approximately one-second point pairs within each camera shot. Requiring two consecutive reliable pairs with more than five source pixels of residual movement yields **15 sustained motion cues**, **32 weak-motion cues**, and **122 abstentions from insufficient or mixed registration**. Among the 108 tracks with a unique prior-area next step, only six have a sustained camera-relative cue, 14 have a weak cue, and 88 abstain. This is evidence coverage, not stone classification accuracy.

The source-reviewed [S390 red stone](norway-cap-ice-context-source-probe/s390-r01-source.jpg) has two reliable moving pairs, but several other visible stones lack enough registered pairs. The sideline-rack S304 has no reliable pair, and the interview-face S321 produces mixed residuals, including one large value, rather than a clean weak-motion classification. At one-second spacing, low-texture ice and broadcast pans often defeat background registration; a foreground person can also move independently of the camera. Thus the existing whole-frame similarity estimate is a useful corroborating cue where supported, but it cannot serve as a hard false-track filter. A stronger version would need rink-plane-specific reference features and explicit foreground/sideboard separation, with abstention when those references are absent.

### Short frame pairs and a restricted background region

The [0.25 s chained audit](norway-subframe-cap-track-registration-development.json) reuses exactly the earlier **1,010** point pairs from all 169 paths. It composes camera transforms only when *every* subinterval satisfies the unchanged registration reliability rule. This raises registered pairs from **396 to 550** and sustained relative-motion cues from **15 to 48**. It also mistakenly gives a sustained cue to the source-reviewed S321 interview face. Better temporal registration alone does not identify a stone.

The [ice-like-region audit](norway-ice-region-cap-track-registration-development.json) repeats those pairs with features restricted to the largest broad low-saturation bright region in each frame, excluding a patch around the tracked target. A 41-pixel morphological close keeps blue rings and printed rink artwork inside the region; the region must cover at least 20% of the image. These are **heuristics selected after inspecting this exposed source**, not a calibrated rink segmentation. Registered pairs rise from **550 to 575** relative to the whole-frame short-pair version, and sustained cues from **48 to 59**; **86 tracks still abstain** because of insufficient or mixed registration. Of the 48 previously sustained paths, 45 remain sustained, two abstain and one becomes weak. Fourteen formerly non-sustained paths gain a sustained cue.

The [frozen transition source packet](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/manifest.json) includes **all three** paths losing a sustained cue and the **four largest-span** newly sustained paths, selected by catalog span before viewing frames. The three losses [S245](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s245-r01-source.jpg), [S321](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s321-r01-source.jpg) and [S326](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s326-r03-source.jpg) visibly track faces. The four gains [S374](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s374-y01-source.jpg), [S327](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s327-r01-source.jpg), [S480](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s480-r01-source.jpg) and [S474](norway-ice-region-registration-source-probe/s474-r01-source.jpg) visibly track moving stones. The remaining transitions were not exhaustively source-reviewed, and none of these selected judgments gives event precision, recall or persistent physical identity.

The scoped short-pair method is a better **source-only motion corroborator** on this recording. The sideline-rack S304 still receives a weak cue rather than a verified rejection, and an ice-like region is not proof that feature matches lie on the rink plane. It should not veto candidates or be promoted into the throw-event score until different camera families and independent event labels are tested. The next step is a view-aware rink-plane model with explicit foreground and sideline exclusion, followed by full-trajectory identity review rather than another single-frame colour gate.
